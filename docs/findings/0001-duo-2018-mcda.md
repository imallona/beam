## Observation

Pooling ARI, runtime and Shannon entropy difference across the twelve datasets and running the beam MCDA pipeline, Seurat ranks first under the default (equal weights, SAW) with a composite of 0.947, ahead of PCAKmeans (0.858) and PCAHC (0.857). Seurat ranks first in all sixteen weighting-by-method configurations tested (equal, entropy, std, critic crossed with saw, topsis, vikor, promethee_ii). SMAA over 2000 Dirichlet weight draws (method topsis) puts Seurat top-ranked in 0.992 of samples, SC3 in 0.008, and no other method first. The smallest-weight-perturbation check finds Seurat's top rank is not fragile. Leave-one-dataset-out (equal weights, SAW) keeps Seurat first in all twelve runs that drop one dataset; the most influential dataset is KohTCC, whose removal moves some rank by at most one position. On ARI alone, the Friedman test over the 12-method complete block is significant (statistic 47.36, p = 1.9e-6), but the Nemenyi critical difference is 4.81, larger than most rank gaps, so the methods sit in overlapping cliques. On the per-dataset ARI ranks alone, SC3 has the best average rank (3.17), with Seurat tied near the top (4.79).

## Method

Loaded the tensor with `beam.datasets.load_duo2018`. Dropped cluster-count deviation (101 of 168 cells missing). For the other three metrics, pooled across the twelve datasets at the example level with a NaN-aware version of each card's `recommended_aggregation_across_datasets`: arithmetic mean for ARI and Shannon entropy difference, geometric mean for runtime, over the observed datasets only (the five gaps fall on SAFE and ascend). Fed the pooled 14 by 3 matrix to `beam.mcda.run_from_registry`, which pulls polarity, declared bounds and per-metric normalization (baseline_relative for ARI, log_min_max for runtime, min_max for entropy difference) from the cards. Sensitivity: the sixteen-configuration rank grid, `smaa` (n_samples 2000, method topsis, seed 0), `smallest_weight_perturbation` (equal weights, SAW), `leave_one_dataset_out` on the full 14 by 12 by 3 tensor through `beam.rank` (the nan-aware reduction keeps all twelve datasets evaluable, since the five missing cells fall on SAFE and ascend, which are still observed on the remaining datasets after any single omission), and `critical_difference` on the complete-ARI block (Friedman with Nemenyi post-hoc at alpha 0.05). The vignette draws the rank heatmap, the critical-difference diagram, and the SMAA confidence bar chart, all axis-labeled with the per-metric ground-truth direction marked.

## Implications

The headline of the original benchmark, that Seurat (and SC3) are strong general choices, reproduces under beam's ontology-aware pipeline, and Seurat's top rank is stable across the weighting and aggregation choices that an analyst would otherwise make by hand. The critical-difference diagram is the useful qualifier: the composite ranks Seurat first because it pools strong runtime and entropy-difference scores alongside ARI, not because it is the single best on ARI. On ARI, SC3 leads and the upper-middle methods are not separable at twelve datasets. Two divergences from the original narrative: the original names RaceID2 and FlowSOM as consistent low performers, but here only RaceID2 ranks near the bottom (composite rank 13 of 14); FlowSOM sits mid-pack (composite rank 9). On ARI alone SC3 has the better average per-dataset rank. The NaN handling is example-level on purpose; coverage-aware pooling inside the core pipeline is future work tied to the heterogeneity module.
